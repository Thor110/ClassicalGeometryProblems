Classical Geometry Problems // Open Archive

A Catalogue of Straightedge and Compass Approximations, Ranked, Open and Reproducible.
Approximations, Not Claims
Every construction here is an approximation, and is labelled as one, with its accuracy measured and its file open to check. Nothing here claims to overturn the proofs that exact solutions are impossible.
Project Purpose
For over two thousand years people have tried to square the circle, trisect an angle and double the cube with nothing but a straightedge and a compass. We now know exact solutions are impossible. The search for close ones never stopped being worthwhile: it is how Hippocrates found his lunes, how Archimedes bounded π, and how Kochański and Ramanujan left constructions we still draw today.
This archive collects those approximations in one place, ranks them on open charts by accuracy and by how many circles and lines they take, and keeps every GeoGebra file so anyone can check, improve or beat them. It also gives journals, mathematicians and forums one place to point people to, kindly, when someone shares a construction.
Trying Is Not Foolish
Searching for these constructions is fun, and it teaches more geometry than most textbooks. Yet people who share an honest, clearly labelled approximation are too often met with ridicule. Nobody would go back in time and call Archimedes, Kochański or Ramanujan fools for trying. This archive takes the other view: measure the attempt, record it, and credit the person who made it.
