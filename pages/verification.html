Classical Geometry Problems // Verification

Who checks the charts, how, and how to ask for a check or dispute one. People and AI are kept apart.
What Verifying Means
To verify an entry is to reproduce it independently and confirm four things:
  1. Accuracy: it gives the charted figure, by the rule 100 − |constructed − true| ÷ true × 100, truncated to the places shown. Work from full-precision values: figures rounded for display can move the result by more than the last charted digit.
  2. Side: it lands oversized or undersized, as charted.
  3. Tools: only straightedge and compass steps lead to the result. No typed lengths or angles, and no point placed by eye, apart from the given figure.
  4. Steps: the circles and lines match the chart under the counting rules.
A verification is evidence anyone can follow: a GeoGebra link, a file, or a written reproduction.
Status Of An Entry
  • Verified reproduced by at least one verifier who is not its method holder, a person or an AI. The chart says which.
  • Self-verified checked only by its own method holder.
  • Unverified submitted, not checked yet.
  • Disputed a dispute is open, shown until it is resolved.
A person's verification carries more weight than an AI check, and the chart always shows which kind each entry has.
Verifiers: People
Anyone can join: verify an entry and post your reproduction in a verification issue. Once it is checked, you are added here and credited on that entry. Verifying your own method counts as self-verification.
Verifiers: AI
The AI check rebuilds each GeoGebra file and recomputes it. It cannot check drawings on paper, count steps under the counting rules, or judge who found a construction first. It may raise a dispute, but people resolve them. It is reproducible: anyone can run python tools/verify.py in the repository and get the same answer.
Second Pair Of Eyes Wanted
These entries have been reproduced by the AI check but not yet by a person, most important first: historical constructions to check against their original source, ones where a garbled or missing part of the source had to be read back from the numbers it gives, and the best on each side of each chart. Step through one, compare it with its notes and source, and post what you find. Ten minutes with one of these is the most useful check anyone can make.
Every Entry
Cross-Reference
Every entry is compared with every other entry of its problem by tools/crossref.py, in two independent ways: whether they produce the same result, and how much of their constructions they share. A construction's fingerprint is every distance between the points its result depends on, and every circle's radius, scaled to the given figure. It does not change when a construction is moved, turned, mirrored or resized, so the same construction drawn twice is found however it was drawn. A new GeoGebra file can be checked against the whole archive before it is added: python tools/crossref.py new.ggb stc.
  • Identical: the same result and the same construction.
  • Same result: the same number, reached a different way.
  • Near identical: most of both constructions is shared.
  • Builds on: one construction lies whole inside another, as an adjustment or extension of it.
  • Shares most steps: most of the smaller construction lies inside the larger.
  • Same file: two squares drawn in one GeoGebra file, expected and listed for completeness.
Requests And Disputes
All requests are GitHub issues, using these forms:
  • Submission: a new approximation.
  • Verification: "I reproduced this entry", or "please check this entry".
  • Dispute: a challenge to an entry's figures or credit.
A dispute may be about accuracy, side, the step count, a step that is not straightedge and compass, credit (someone found it first; the entry carries the ~ mark meanwhile), or a duplicate. It runs like this:
  1. It is opened with evidence: the reproduction, the values found, and how.
  2. The entry shows Disputed while it is open, and anyone may add a reproduction for or against.
  3. The maintainer decides, giving the reason in the issue. Upheld: the figures are corrected (an entry is only removed if it falls below 95% or is a duplicate). Rejected: it stands. Credit: the discoverer is credited.
  4. A decision can be reopened with new evidence.
Disputes are about constructions and numbers, never about people. Mockery or personal remarks get an issue closed.

    AI-Assisted Submissions And AI Method Holders
    If an AI helped find or refine a construction, say so in the submission's notes. That keeps the record honest, and it is not held against the submission.
    An AI system may also hold a method it found itself. It is listed under its name with an AI label on the chart, kept apart from people, and its entries need a person's verification like any other. An AI never verifies its own finds alone: its automated check is recorded, but the entry stays self-verified until someone else reproduces it.
