Classical Geometry Problems // Submit An Approximation

Found a construction? Measure it, write it up, and add it to the charts. Every honest attempt is welcome.
How To Submit
  1. Draw your construction in GeoGebra using only circles and lines: straightedge and compass, no measuring.
  2. Measure the constructed value and the true value with GeoGebra's tools, and work out the accuracy with the calculator below.
  3. Fill in the form below. It writes your submission in the archive's template.
  4. Send it straight from GeoGebra with the extension, or open the submission form with the button below (it fills in what it can), or send a pull request adding your files to Submissions/. Include a public GeoGebra link so anyone can check it.
  5. Your entry is then verified under the verification rules, by people and by the automated check, and its record is shown on the chart.
Not sure it's good enough? If it reaches 95%, submit it. A first attempt at 95% teaches as much as a record, and every attempt from 95% up has a place on the charts.
Submit Straight From GeoGebra
The archive's browser extension sends a construction to the archive from the GeoGebra page it is open on. It works in Chrome, Edge, Brave, Opera and other Chromium browsers.
  1. Open your construction on geogebra.org, with the true value measured (the circle's area or circumference, the arc's length, the whole angle) as well as your result.
  2. Click the extension. It reads the construction from that tab, guesses the problem and the two measurements, and previews the accuracy. Change any guess that is wrong.
  3. Give your real name; it is published with your construction, permanently. Method name, description and notes are optional.
  4. Tick the box if an AI helped, and the box to confirm it is your own work (or that you credit its discoverer).
  5. Click Submit As A Pull Request. The first time, it asks you to sign in to GitHub with a short code. Your construction is added to Submissions/ in your own copy of the archive and a pull request is opened.
No GitHub account? Download As Zip saves the same three files, and Open As An Issue opens a prefilled submission form.
What Happens Next
  • Within a few minutes the archive rebuilds your construction from its own geometry, confirms which problem it solves, measures it to 32 digits, counts its circles and lines, checks for points placed by hand, micro scale steps and shallow crossings, and compares it with every entry already on the charts. The result is posted as a comment on your pull request.
  • Ready for review: the maintainer verifies it and adds it to the charts.
  • Needs a manual check: it did not fit the problem chosen, the problem could not be identified, or something needs a person to look at it. Nothing is rejected for this; it waits for the maintainer.
  • Possible duplicate: it matches an entry already on the charts. If it is the same construction, your name is added to that entry under Also found by, as a possible independent rediscovery; the same result reached by different steps is a method of its own and is charted separately.
  • Closed straight away: a second copy of a construction already waiting (a double click), more than five of your submissions waiting at once, or wording that does not pass the archive's language check. Your name must be your real name, since the discovery is credited to it.
  • Below 95%: the charts start at 95%, so the extension will not send it and the pull request is closed with its figures. Many charted methods began further out. Keep refining it; small changes often help a lot.

The Ruleset
  • Base ruleset: at most 25 circles and 40 lines.
  • Extended ruleset: at most 49 circles and 80 lines, for constructions at 99.91% and above.
  • Range: 95% minimum, 100% maximum.
  • Counted: only the circles and lines the result depends on. Not counted: exploration, intersections, macro scale midpoints, the given figure, and the result itself. See the counting rules.
  • Key marks: ~ contested, @ too simple, µ micro scale edits.
If your GeoGebra file gives a more precise readout than three decimal places, include it as well. Full ruleset.
