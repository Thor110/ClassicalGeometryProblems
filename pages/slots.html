Classical Geometry Problems // Open Slots

Every budget of circles and lines is a slot on each side of a chart. Which are filled, which are open, and how many ways there are to draw.
Each slot is one budget, exactly c circles and l lines, on one side of the chart, and it has one best construction to find: the most accurate that fits the budget. With at most C circles and L lines there are (C + 1) × (L + 1) slots per side.
Slots Grid
Circles run left to right, lines top to bottom. Hover or tap a slot for its budget and best entry; click a filled slot to open the entry on its chart. Each step of shade is ten times closer to perfect, as on the ladder.
Key
  • Filled: an entry on the chart uses exactly this budget, shaded by its accuracy (gold oversized, blue undersized; brighter is closer to perfect).
  • On the frontier: the best entry for this budget or anything cheaper. To take its place, a construction must be at least as cheap and more accurate.
  • Shared: more than one entry uses this budget; the slot shows the best.
  • Open, nothing this cheap yet: no entry at this budget or below. These look like the easiest slots, and some may be, but the smallest budgets may have no construction of 95% or better at all: nobody has shown that one exists, or that none does. A construction here would join the frontier at once.
  • Open, beat what is cheaper: a cheaper construction already exists. Any 95% construction fills the slot, but to matter it should beat the best cheaper one, which hovering shows.
  • Cannot be filled: shown to be impossible, and left out of the open count.
    • No circles. A straightedge alone, from the given points, can only draw lines through them, and those lines meet only at the given points again: no new point, so no new length or angle.
    • One circle, for squaring and rectifying the circle. That circle is the given circle itself, counted as the rules require. Lines can then only join the centre and the points on one diameter, all on one line, so the only lengths are the radius and the diameter: at best 72.7% of the circle's area, and far less of its circumference.
Open slots are switched off at first, so the filled pattern reads first. The rulesets, and the reasoning behind the count of ways to draw, are in the ruleset.
