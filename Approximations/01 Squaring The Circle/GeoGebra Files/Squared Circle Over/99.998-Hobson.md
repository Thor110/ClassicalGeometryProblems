**Description:**
Kunze (1842): "Man theilt AC in fünf gleiche Theile; AD sind zwei davon. Eben so BC in zwei gleiche Theile; einen davon, BE, trägt man auf den verlängerten Durchmesser nach BF. Beschreibt man nun über DE einen Halbkreis, der GH in G schneidet; ingleichen über AF einen Halbkreis, der GH in H schneidet: so ist GH die Seite eines Quadrats, das sehr nahe dem Kreise gleich ist." Here C is the centre.

In Hobson's words: "Let AOB be the diameter of a given circle. Let OD = 3/5 r, OF = 3/2 r, OE = 1/2 r. Describe the semi-circles DGE, AHF with DE and AF as diameters; and let the perpendicular to AB through O cut them in G and H respectively. The square of which the side is GH is approximately of area equal to that of the circle."

OG is the mean proportional of OD and OE, √(3/10) of the radius, and OH that of OA and OF, √(3/2), so GH = √(6/5) · φ of the radius and its square is 6/5 · (1 + φ) = 3.14164079 of the radius squared. OD = 3/5 r is laid off with five radii up the perpendicular and a parallel; OF = 3/2 r with half a radius beyond B; OE = 1/2 r is the midpoint of OA. The square is raised on GH.

**Notes / Limitations:**
Hobson gives it without naming its author, and it was charted as Hobson's construction until Kunze's book of 1842 was found. The scans of Hobson's book garble the three fractions; Kunze's text gives them plainly: OD = 3/5, OE = 1/2 and OF = 3/2 of the radius. The same value, 6/5 · (1 + φ), was found again by Robert Dixon (Mathographics, 1991), and H. V. Chu ("Squaring the Circle Revisited", 2019, arXiv:1908.01202) gives a shorter construction of it: with AB = 1 and BC = 2 at right angles, AD = AC = √5 on AB extended, DM = 2/5 AD and ND = DM/2, the circle on NB as diameter, and MH perpendicular to NB; MH² = 6/5 · (1 + φ). It gives exactly the same square as Kunze's, so it is noted here rather than charted again.
