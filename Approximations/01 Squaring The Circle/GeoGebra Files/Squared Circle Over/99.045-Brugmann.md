**Description:**
Brugmann: "Sit quadrandus circulus ABCD ex centro E descriptus. Dividatur aequaliter in partes quatuor ... Distinguatur iterum una quaedam ex his quatuor partibus semidiametro quinta, etiam ex centro E in peripheriae punctum F producta ... Ducatur deinde recta ex A in B, quae ubi semidiametrum EF secabit, ibi notetur G. Similiter ducatur linea altera ex A in F, tertia ex A in H, quarta etiam ex H in I, hac ratione, ut F.G.H.I.E distantia aequali separentur." From the lunule AKF being equal to the triangle HIE he concludes that the rectangle of the diameter and of the diameter less EH equals the circle.

In the file Brugmann's E is O, and his quarter AB is the arc from C to Bt; F is on the bisector of the right angle, G where the chord CBt meets it, and H where the circle about G through F meets it again. The rectangle's sides, 2r and 2r − EH, are laid end to end along the x axis (from A, through C, to P), and the semicircle on AP cuts the perpendicular at C at Q: CQ is the side of the square.

**Notes / Limitations:**
Over by 0.95%. Brugmann leaves the area as a rectangle; turning it into a square is a standard step added here.
