**Description:**
Anglin: "Let ABCD be circumscribed square, HE, EF two sides of inscribed square of given circle, AC, BD diagonals of squares. With centre E and radius EA describe a circle cutting sides of inscribed square in I and J; join IJ and produce it to meet circle at N. Then the line ONK, drawn through N parallel to BC and terminated by the diagonals at O and K, is nearly equal to the side of a square equal in area to the given circle." His working: "NO = √2 − 1. Thus ONK = √2 − 1 + √(2√2 − 1) = 1.76640701."

In the file: the square about the circle from its four tangents, and its diagonals; E, K and L corners of the inscribed square on them, so EK and EL are the inscribed square's sides through E. The circle about E with the circle's radius cuts them at I and J; IJ meets the circle at P (Anglin's N); the parallel to the top side through P meets the diagonals at Q and R (his O and K), and the square is raised on QR.

**Notes / Limitations:**
