**Description:**
Thibaut's translation: "If you wish to turn a circle into a square, divide the diameter into eight parts, and again one of these eight parts into twenty-nine parts; of these twenty-nine parts remove twenty-eight and moreover the sixth part (of the one left part) less the eighth part (of the sixth part)." That is, the side is 7/8 + 1/(8·29) − 1/(8·29·6) + 1/(8·29·6·8) of the diameter.

In the file AB is the diameter. Two midpoints give R, seven eighths of the way from A. On the perpendicular at R the length RB is stepped out 29 times (by doubling and adding), and the parallel through the first step to the line from the 29th to B cuts off RT, a twenty-ninth of RB. The same steps divide RT into sixths (V at five sixths), and three midpoints give the eighth of a sixth, which is laid on from V to X. The square is raised on AX.

**Notes / Limitations:**
Under by 1.7% in area. The rule is arithmetic: Thibaut shows it comes from Baudhāyana's rule for turning a square into a circle, written in whole numbers. Its last two corrections are about a thousandth and a ten-thousandth of the diameter, far below the micro scale threshold, so the entry carries the µ mark. They do not help: the first two terms alone, 7/8 + 1/232, give 98.45%, and the corrections take it back to 98.30%, because the numbers they come from (1224 : 1393) are themselves the square-into-circle rule, which is 1.7% out.
