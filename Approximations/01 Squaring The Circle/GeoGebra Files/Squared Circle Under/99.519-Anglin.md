**Description:**
Anglin: "Repeat the circle as in last case by arcs AEO and AFO, and let I be the intersection of FE and the diagonal ABO (fig. 10). Join CI, and with centre C and radius CI describe a circle cutting the arc AEO in J. Then the line through J parallel to AN and terminated by the diagonals of the circumscribed square is very nearly equal to the side of a square equal in area to the given circle."

In the file, as in his third: the circles about the points of contact M and N, with the circle's radius (his arcs AEO and AFO, through the corner and the centre), meet the lines of the inscribed square's sides at E and F. FE meets the diagonal at I. C′ is where the inscribed square's side through E meets the side of the square about the circle (his C, read from the plate). The circle about C′ through I meets the arc about M at J; the parallel to the side through J meets the diagonals at Q and R, and the square is raised on QR.

**Notes / Limitations:**
