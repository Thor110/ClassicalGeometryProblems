**Description:**
Anglin: "Let two adjacent sides of the circumscribed square touch the circle at N and H. Repeat the circle from centres N and H by arcs cutting sides of the inscribed square in F and E; join FE and mark off its fourth part FI on FB. Then if on the diagonal AC we mark off AK equal to IB, the line KM parallel to AH and terminated by the other diagonal at M is very nearly the side of a square equal in area to the given circle." His working gives FE = √(2√2 − 1) − 1 and IB = ¼{√(2√2 − 1) − 1}(2√2 − 1), so KM = 2 − √2·IB = 1.77232578.

In the file N and M are the points of contact on two neighbouring sides, and K the corner of the inscribed square between them (Anglin's B). The circle about M meets the line of the inscribed square's side through K at E, and the circle about N meets the other side's line at F, both beyond K. FI, a quarter of FE (two midpoints), is laid off along FK; IK is laid off from the corner B of the square about the circle along the diagonal to X (his AK); the parallel to the side BM through X meets the other diagonal at Y, and the square is raised on XY.

**Notes / Limitations:**
Short by 0.000128 of the radius, nearly a fifteen-thousandth of the side, as Anglin says. E and F lie on the inscribed square's sides produced, not on the sides themselves; that is the only reading that gives his figures.
