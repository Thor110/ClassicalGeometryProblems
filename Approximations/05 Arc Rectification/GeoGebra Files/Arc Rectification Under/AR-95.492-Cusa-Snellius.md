**Description:**
Yates: "extend the diameter BE of the unit circle to P so that EP = 1. The line PA strikes the tangent at D. From the similar triangles PCA and PBD ... BD = 3 sin θ/(2 + cos θ). Thus the approximation made by von Cusa is that of replacing the arc length subtending angle AOB by the tangent length BD."

The arc is BA about O. The circle about E through O gives P on the diameter, a radius beyond E; the tangent at B is the perpendicular to OB there.

**Notes / Limitations:**
A general method for arcs up to a quarter circle, and very good for small ones; past 90° it falls away fast (82% at 120°, 50% at 150°). Charted at 90°, its weakest angle in the 0-90° range Yates tabulates. Cusa took 3 sin θ/(2 + cos θ) as exact; Snellius showed it is a lower bound, and Huygens proved it. Snellius' matching upper bound needs a neusis, so is not a straightedge and compass construction. Yates also uses BD to trisect the angle (BM = BD/3, PM meets the circle at T), but that trisection falls under 95% beyond about 75°.
