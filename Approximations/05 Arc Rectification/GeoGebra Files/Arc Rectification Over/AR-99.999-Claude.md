**Description:**
Rankine's second rectification (see the historical entry), worked on the arc halved four times and the length multiplied back:

1. Bisect the angle four times from B: H₁ to H₄, so the arc BH₄ is a sixteenth of the arc BA.
2. On the arc BH₄: a quarter of the arc BH₄ by two more bisections, to E; the radius OE meets the tangent at B in C; the circle about C through H₄ carries CH₄ on along the tangent to X, so that BX = BC + CH₄.
3. Double the length four times along the tangent at B: the circle about the end through B meets the tangent again at twice the distance; repeat from that point. The last, sixteen times the first, is the constructed length.

**Notes / Limitations:**
A general method, charted at its weakest angle from 30° to 150°. Its smallest step is 9.4% of the radius; halved a fifth time it would fall to 4.7%, micro scale. Credit for the construction is Rankine's; only the halving is new.
