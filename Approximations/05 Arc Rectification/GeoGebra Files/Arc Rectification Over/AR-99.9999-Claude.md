**Description:**
Rankine's two rectifications combined (see the historical entry), worked on the arc halved four times and the length multiplied back:

1. Bisect the angle four times from B: H₁ to H₄, so the arc BH₄ is a sixteenth of the arc BA.
2. On the arc BH₄: both of Rankine's constructions, laid on the tangent at B (D from the first, X from the second), and Y a fifth of the way from X to D (five radii along the perpendicular at X, the line from the fifth to D and the parallel through the first), so BY = (4 BX + BD)/5.
3. Double the length four times along the tangent at B: the circle about the end through B meets the tangent again at twice the distance; repeat from that point. The last, sixteen times the first, is the constructed length.

**Notes / Limitations:**
A general method, charted at its weakest angle from 30° to 150°. Rankine's combination cancels the fifth-order errors of his two constructions and leaves a seventh-order one, so each halving gains about 128 times. Its smallest step is 6.2% of the radius; halved a fifth time it would be micro scale. Credit for the construction is Rankine's; only the halving is new.
