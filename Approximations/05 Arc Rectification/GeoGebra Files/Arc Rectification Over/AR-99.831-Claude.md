**Description:**
Starting from the arc BA about O:

1. Draw the circle about O through B; the line OB meets it again at E, and the circle about E through O gives P, a radius beyond E (Snellius' point, as in Cusa's rectification).
2. Drop the perpendicular from A to OB, meeting it at C: BC is the versine of the arc.
3. Take a quarter of BC by two midpoints: M₁ the midpoint of BC, M₂ the midpoint of BM₁.
4. Move P towards O by BM₂, to P'.
5. Draw the tangent at B, and the line from P' through A, meeting the tangent at D.

BD is the constructed length: sin x (3 − v/4) / (2 − v/4 + cos x) of the radius, where v = 1 − cos x.

**Notes / Limitations:**
Lambert moves P by a fifth of the versine, which gives Newton's formula and is best for small arcs; a quarter gives up a little there (99.990% at 30° against Lambert's 99.999%) for much more on larger ones (99.96% at 90° against 99.03%, and 96.5% at 120° against 93.0%). Its weakest point up to 90° is 76.17°, where it is drawn. Credit for the construction is Lambert's (and Cusa's and Snellius'); only the fraction is new.
