**Description:**
Yates: "A comparative study of the arc lengths of a particular circle and the trisecting Limaçon of Pascal led Kopf in 1919 to the following method, which was refined somewhat later by Perron and d'Ocagne. The unit circle cuts the sides of the given angle at A and B. The midpoint D of OC is marked, PC is taken equal to 1, and DF as one-third the length DE; that is, since OD = 1/2, OE = 1: DE = √3/2 and DF = √3/6. With center at F: (−(3 + √3)/6, 0) and radius FB = 1 + (3 + √3)/6, the arc BA′ is constructed. The line CA meets this arc in A′ and angle A′PB is approximately ∠AOB/3."

In the file: C is where the circle meets the diameter through B, beyond O; D is the middle of OC and P a radius beyond C. E is where the perpendicular at D meets the circle. DE is divided into thirds by the parallel to BE through O (D, O, the middle of OB and B are evenly spaced), and DF is laid off along the diameter. The arc about F through B meets CA at A′. The parallel to PA′ through O meets the circle at S; the rest, SOA, is bisected.

**Notes / Limitations:**
Charted at 63.95°, its weakest angle up to 90°, the range of Perron's table; it is exact at 90°. On larger angles it weakens: 99.60% at 150°. The figure in Yates is the refined form; Kopf's original of 1919 and Perron's paper of 1933 are not in the archive's sources.

The Jahrbuch über die Fortschritte der Mathematik (JFM 60.1233.02) describes Perron's 1933 paper step for step, and it is this construction; Nehring's largest error, 14.86" at 69.934°, is what the rebuilt figure gives. Yates' date of 1919 is for Kopf's earliest work. Kopf's first published construction (1929) is a different one, with errors up to 8' 12".
