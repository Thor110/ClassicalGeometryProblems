# Wanted: historical constructions, in progress

The charts list known historical approximations that are not charted yet, under "Wanted" on each
chart. A construction is charted under a historical name only once it has been checked against the
original source, or a faithful published description of it, step by step, so that the credit is for
what that person actually drew. Drafts in progress are kept here.

If a historical construction turns out to be one already charted under a later name, the credit moves to
the earliest author, and the later finder stays on the entry under "Also found by", as a possible
independent rediscovery ([Earlier discoveries](../SUBMISSION_RULESET.md#earlier-discoveries)). A
historical method that only reaches the same value by other steps is charted beside it, as Stifel's is
beside Gordon's Cube 2 Tesseract v1.0 (both exactly 5/4 of the edge).

Found in the Jahrbuch but not charted:

| Construction | Why |
|---|---|
| Kopf's first trisection (1929) | The review leaves the distance of the two circles open, and no reading gives Perron's largest error, 8' 12". Sauerbeck's (1951) has the same largest error and may be the same construction. |
| Descartes' isoperimetric squaring (Œuvres posthumes; Montucla 1831, pp. 272-273) | An endless series of rectangles; stopped after any number of steps it is an approximation, but Descartes gives no stopping point. |
| Oronce Finé (1556): the circumference as the smaller of two mean proportionals between the perimeters of the inscribed and circumscribed squares | Two mean proportionals cannot be drawn with ruler and compass; Finé's own construction for them is needed (De rebus mathematicis hactenus desideratis, [ARes43323](https://archive.org/details/ARes43323)). |
| Bryson (5th century BC): the circle as the mean of the inscribed and circumscribed squares | Known only from later commentators, and read in different ways (Montucla takes the arithmetic mean, π = 3). |
| Longomontanus (1644), Scaliger (1594), Van Eyk (1584) and other claimed quadratures | Their values are reported (3.14185 for Longomontanus), but not yet the constructions; Longomontanus' book is on the Internet Archive ([ARes744233](https://archive.org/details/ARes744233)), as is Buteo's survey of 1559 ([ARes13633](https://archive.org/details/ARes13633)). |
| Bouvelles' arcufication (a line made into a quadrant) and Uffenbach's later figures | These turn a line into an arc, or roll a wheel; the charts take the circle as given. |
| Huygens' bounds for an arc from its chord and sine (1654, Theorem XVI and Problem IV) | Numerical bounds, worked as fourth proportionals for the ratio of circumference to diameter; no construction is given. |
| Selmer (1957), Finsler (1959), Schröder (1957), Srůtek (1892), Jičínský (1872) | The reviews give the value only (8 − √62 for ∛2/10; 3/2 + √97/6; and others). |
| Kopf-style hyperbola and limaçon substitutes (Kübl 1906, Franceschi 1913, Hartmann 1938) | Families of constructions; the reviews give the error formulas, not one construction to draw. |
| d'Ocagne's trisection of 1931 (HD = HB/3, DC = 2HB) | Under 95% at 90° (94.7%). |
| Bilimowitsch's rectification (1908) | Good for small arcs (error θ⁵/20), but under 90% by 60°. |
| Reichenbächer (1901), Sorgner (1915, 1917), Heiseler (1935), Kerzdörfer (1938), Harris (1933) and others | The reviews give a value or a claim, not the construction. |

## Variants

`tools/variants.py` keeps a historical construction step for step and tries other fractions in it; its findings are in
[Variants/](../Variants/README.md). One is charted so far: Lambert's rectification with a quarter of the versine instead
of a fifth (arc rectification, 99.831% over, at 76.17°), credited to Lambert's method and found by the search.
Two more, the same way: Cusa's trisection with the point moved a quarter of the versine (trisecting an angle, 99.660%
under, at 90°), and Kunze's square on GH (the construction Hobson gives) with OD = 1/3, OE = 11/8 and OF = 6/5 (squaring the circle, 99.9993% under).

The search also shows how far the historical authors' own choices hold up: for most of them no other small fraction
does better (Hayden, Kunze, Willich, Buonafalce's first, the Rhind papyrus, Kochański, Specht, de Gelder, Karajordanoff,
d'Ocagne and Dürer, with only halving fractions finer than a sixteenth beating some of them, at micro scale).

Worked on the angle halved again and again and then multiplied back (a line and a circle for each halving), the
historical trisections and arc rectifications hold from 30° to 150° about as well as they do on small angles. Each is
charted at the deepest halving whose steps stay at macro scale: Cusa's rectification halved five times (99.99997%) and
his trisection four times (99.9996%), Schoeler's (d'Ocagne's) four times, Karajordanoff's three, the quarter-versine trisection twice,
Vahlen's, Lambert's and the quarter-versine rectification once, and Dürer's from one side (with his halved once, at
micro scale). The simplest methods go furthest, because they have no small fractions of their own. Ramanujan's 1913 and
1914 constructions are at their best already, as is Schoeler's (d'Ocagne's) point: its exact place drifts only a fiftieth of the
versine. The full table is in [Variants/](../Variants/README.md).
