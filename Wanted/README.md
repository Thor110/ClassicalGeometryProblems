# Wanted: historical constructions, in progress

The charts list known historical approximations that are not charted yet, under "Wanted" on each
chart. A construction is charted under a historical name only once it has been checked against the
original source (or a faithful published description of it) step by step, so that the credit is for
what that person actually drew.

| Durer-trisection-draft.ggb | Albrecht Dürer (1525): an approximate trisection, 99.396% at 150° | Dürer's method as modern accounts describe it (G. H. Hughes, "The Polygons of Albrecht Dürer", 2012; Division by Zero, 2011): trisect the chord; raise perpendiculars at the two points to the arc; swing those back down to the chord from its ends; take the point two thirds of the way across each small gap; swing back up to the arc. Built fresh, it gives 99.99996% at 30°, 99.997% at 60°, 99.97% at 90°, 99.84% at 120° and 99.40% at 150°. 12 circles and 9 lines. To be checked against Dürer's figure in "Underweysung der Messung" (1525). Before charting, the archive's angle sweep needs to follow this figure more reliably: as it narrows below about 40°, the sweep loses track of the right crossing points and reports less than the construction really gives. |
